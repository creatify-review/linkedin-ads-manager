## What it is

Campaign Manager is LinkedIn's advertising platform. LinkedIn's product page describes it as the place to create, measure and optimise ad campaigns all in one place, reaching what LinkedIn calls the world's largest professional audience of over 1.2 billion professionals. It is reached at `linkedin.com/campaignmanager`, and it sits behind your normal LinkedIn login - the raw campaign manager URL shows nothing useful until you are signed in, which is why LinkedIn maintains a separate marketing sign-in page that explains the flow before sending you through.

The platform is organised around a two-step start that LinkedIn spells out on every advertising page: first create a LinkedIn Page for your company, to build the brand and trust with your community; second, create your first campaign in Campaign Manager. Inside, you choose an ad format, define targeting, and then measure and optimise from the same interface. LinkedIn also runs a Marketing Academy with courses and certifications, a Help Center for the product, and a Success Hub of resources, all linked from the advertising navigation.

## How to get started

1. **Create a LinkedIn Page** for the business if it does not have one. LinkedIn lists this as step one; campaigns are attached to a Page.
2. **Sign in to Campaign Manager** at `linkedin.com/campaignmanager` with the LinkedIn account that administers the Page. LinkedIn's advertising site also offers a `Create ad` entry point that lands on the accounts view.
3. **Follow the How to Create a Campaign guide** under Getting Started on LinkedIn's advertising site; it walks the objective, audience, format and budget steps.
4. **Pick an ad format** from the four LinkedIn lists on its navigation: Sponsored Content, Sponsored Messaging, Dynamic Ads and Text Ads (details below).
5. **Set targeting** using the professional attributes LinkedIn describes on its Targeting page, and check creative dimensions against the Ad Specs guide before uploading.

## Pricing

LinkedIn keeps costs on a dedicated Pricing page under Learn More, with the one-line pitch to discover how much it costs to advertise on LinkedIn. The crawled pages do not print rates, minimums or bid models, so this guide will not either; read the Pricing page and the Help Center for current figures before setting a budget.

## Ad formats, as LinkedIn describes them

- **Sponsored Content** - reach and engage a professional audience in the LinkedIn feed.
- **Sponsored Messaging** - engage prospects where professional conversations happen.
- **Dynamic Ads** - ads automatically personalised to the person seeing them.
- **Text Ads** - self-service formats to create campaigns in minutes.

LinkedIn also offers **Boosting**, described as an easy way to reach new audiences by amplifying your best content, which is a lighter path than a full campaign, and a **Lead Generation** track for collecting qualified leads from the audience.

## Practical notes

1. **The Page comes first.** If you are setting up for a client, get admin access to their LinkedIn Page before you touch Campaign Manager; LinkedIn's own start sequence assumes it exists.
2. **Bookmark the real URL.** `linkedin.com/campaignmanager` is the product; `business.linkedin.com/advertise/sign-in` is a marketing page about signing in. Both end in the same place, but the first is faster.
3. **Check specs before creative review.** The Ad Specs guide (the same page LinkedIn calls Compare Ads) covers every format. Fixing dimensions after a client sign-off costs a round of approvals.
4. **Boost before you build, when appropriate.** If a Page post already performs organically, Boosting is the lower-effort test LinkedIn offers; save a full campaign for creative that has not been validated.
5. **Use the Marketing Academy for onboarding.** LinkedIn's courses and certifications are linked from the advertising site and are the fastest way to get a new team member fluent in the interface.
6. **Agencies and small businesses have separate landing tracks.** LinkedIn maintains distinct pages for each; the agency track covers multi-account work and partners.

## FAQ

**Is Ads Manager the same as Campaign Manager?** Yes. LinkedIn's product name is Campaign Manager; ads manager is what most people type.

**Do I need a company Page to advertise?** LinkedIn lists creating a Page as the first step and Campaign Manager as the second, so plan on having one.

**Which format should I start with?** LinkedIn positions Text Ads as the self-service format you can set up in minutes and Sponsored Content as the feed format. Start with the one that matches the creative you already have.

**Where are the ad dimensions?** The Ad Specs guide under Getting Started on LinkedIn's advertising site.

**Is there an API?** The crawled pages describe the platform through its web interface and its Partners programme; nothing there documents a public API. Treat integrations as a partner conversation.
